How wellbeing burden is allocated

The national wellbeing index is calculated from scored public discourse using the same method as National Analysis (annual bins, no smoothing, sensitivity 5). Higher index values mean better wellbeing.

For each year, we invert the national wellbeing index (1 − wellbeing) and allocate this burden across counties. A county’s weight is its recorded flood count multiplied by its population density, divided by the sum of those weights across Ireland. Allocated burden = (1 − national wellbeing) × county weight. The county allocations sum to the inverse of the national index.

This is an illustrative allocation, not a measured county wellbeing score or an estimate of the causal effect of flooding. Darker colours indicate greater allocated burden. Counties with no recorded floods remain visible in the lightest colour, with zero allocated burden; this does not establish an absence of flooding or harm. With no recorded floods or no discourse, allocation is unavailable. The colour scale stays fixed across years.

Population is held at CSO Census 2022 levels. Density uses approximate areas from the map boundaries; city and county areas are combined into 26 counties. The shared timeline covers years present in both datasets; 2010 discourse begins on 30 March. Flood records: OPW.

Event window

Click anywhere on the precipitation record below to centre the window on that date. Named storms and major floods are marked on it; a chosen day inside one is labelled, but any day can be chosen. The rainfall record is monthly — the finest the data goes — so the choosing is monthly while the window it opens is daily.

Day 0 is the selected date, marked on every plot. For a named storm it defaults to the last day of its impact period — a proxy for the rainfall peak, which lags the peak winds a storm is named for. The before/after comparison needs both sides, so it is only produced for the double-sided window.

Reading these panels

Scores are binned per day and the constructs are shown z-normalised. Standardisation uses the whole corpus, not the window — re-centring inside 31 days would measure each post against its own neighbours and erase exactly the shift being looked for. The climate reference is monthly, so inside a 31-day window it is one or two points; its three panels are therefore drawn compactly, side by side, as steps rather than curves — a monthly total holds across its month and does not slope through it.

DigiÉire

Ireland's flood-wellbeing digital twin

Informed by the international debate for a ‘beyond GDP’ framing of climate change within policy, the goal of this project is to produce a Digital Twin for Ireland to aid climate-change adaptation planning that maximises the wellbeing of the Irish population over time.

What makes it a twin rather than a model

Large-scale analysis of public discourse

The wellbeing side is not a survey of a few hundred people after the fact. It is 90,567 scored discourses spanning 2010–2026, read for distress, functional disruption and alienation, and lined up against the rainfall record and a dated catalogue of Irish storms and floods.

That gives the twin something most simulations lack: a measured response to real floods, at a scale and a time resolution that lets the model be calibrated against what actually happened rather than against an assumption about what people feel.

Projection to 2100 under RCP scenarios

The twin is run forward to 2100 under Representative Concentration Pathways — the standard low, intermediate and high forcing scenarios (RCP 2.6, 4.5 and 8.5) that drive the sea-level and rainfall projections adaptation planning is built on.

Each adaptation option is put through the same population under the same pathways, so the comparison between options is the output. A model that produced one number for one future would not be a twin; being re-runnable under a different future is the whole point.

Measuring wellbeing, concretely

“Wellbeing” only becomes measurable once it is broken into things a reader could disagree with. We use three negative-pole constructs, each scored 0–3 on a piece of public discourse, and combine them into a single index where higher means better.
